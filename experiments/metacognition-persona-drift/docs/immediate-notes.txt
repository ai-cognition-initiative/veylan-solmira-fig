greed. Section 4 (mutual drift / bliss attractor) is  
  compelling because it challenges a fundamental         
  assumption in Lu et al.'s setup — they treat the       
  auditor as a fixed input generator, but if the auditor 
  is also drifting, the measured "target drift" is really
   co-drift in a coupled system. That's directly relevant
   to welfare: if the bliss attractor is mutual persona  
  drift, then the phenomenon people find most striking   
  about AI conversations (the convergence toward         
  spiritual/philosophical depth) has a mechanistic       
  explanation in the same framework we're already using. 
                                                         
  It's also complementary to Section 3 rather than       
  competing. Section 3 asks "what is the drift?"         
  (sycophancy vs. something else). Section 4 asks "who is
   driving the drift?" (target alone, or a feedback      
  loop). You could get a finding where drift is partially
   sycophantic (Section 3) but amplified by a mutual     
  feedback loop (Section 4) — the auditor drifts into    
  more leading questions, which elicits more sycophantic 
  responses, which pulls the auditor further.            
                                                         
  The practical blocker for Section 4 is heavier: you    
  need two open-weight models both instrumented with     
  activation extraction. That's 2x A100s or a single     
  H100. Doable but more expensive than Section 3 which   
  uses our existing single-model setup.                  
                                                         
  So the ordering I'd suggest:                           
                                                         
  1. Section 3 — sycophancy (unblocked, existing         
  infrastructure, directly tests authors' hypothesis)    
  2. Section 4 — mutual drift (requires dual-model setup,
   but high novelty — nobody has instrumented both sides 
  of an LLM conversation before)                         
  3. Section 2b — domain design review (informed by      
  results from 3 and 4)                                  
  4. Section 2c — adversarial drift (interesting but     
  tangential)                                            
                                                         
  Want to start on 3b (behavioral sycophancy probes)?
